Your parents' home should never feel empty.

Savera is a small, screen-free companion that talks with your parents in Hindi, every single day. Morning check-ins, medicine reminders, and your voice notes — carried home over WhatsApp.

Why not just a phone call?

Because a call can't be there for the other 167 hours of the week.

Everyone says "just call home more." Savera exists because that honest advice quietly fails, for reasons nobody's fault.

01

A call needs two free people

You get busy. They don't want to disturb you. The weekly ten-minute call slips to fortnightly, and both sides pretend it's fine.

02

Loneliness doesn't keep office hours

The empty feeling isn't at 7pm on Sunday when you call. It's Tuesday afternoon, Thursday morning, every quiet hour in between.

03

Smartphones weren't built for 75-year-old hands

Small screens, locked phones, forgotten icons, missed video calls. Savera has no screen and no app. You just talk to it, and it talks back.

How it works

No app for them to learn. No screen for them to read.

Savera sits quietly in their home and keeps the day company, in the language they think in.

They just talk

One button, or simply their voice. Savera speaks Hindi warmly and patiently, at whatever pace the conversation needs.

It keeps the day on track

A good-morning check-in, dawa reminders on time, and small conversation in between. Presence, not surveillance.

You stay in the loop

Updates and voice notes travel over WhatsApp, the app your family already lives in. No new account, no dashboard to check.

The device

Made for hands that have worked for fifty years.

Nothing to charge every night, nothing to update, nothing to figure out. Savera is a small warm object that does one thing well: it talks, and it listens.

📵

No screen, ever

Nothing to read, tap, or squint at. Its only face is a soft light that glows while it listens.

🗣️

Speaks their language

Warm, unhurried Hindi that waits for the sentence to finish. More Indian languages on the bench.

👆

One button, that's all

Press and speak — or just call out "Savera" from across the room.

🔌

Set up once, then forgotten

Family plugs it in and connects it to home WiFi on the first visit. After that it simply stays on, like a lamp.

A day with Savera

What a Tuesday looks like now

8:00 AM

Good morning, properly

Savera greets them, asks how they slept, and chats for a few minutes. You get a quiet note that the day has started well.

“Namaste! Aaj subah kaise uthe? Chai ho gayi?”
2:00 PM

Dawa, on time, without nagging from 1,000 km away

Medicine reminders arrive as a friendly voice, not an alarm to be silenced and forgotten. You hear when it's taken.

“Dopahar wali dawa ka time ho gaya. Paani ke saath le lijiye.”
7:30 PM

Your voice, in their living room

You record a voice note on WhatsApp between meetings. Savera plays it at home in the evening, and carries their reply back to you.

“Beta ka message aaya hai. Suniye…”

For the family

The whole thing runs through WhatsApp. That's the point.

No new app to install, no password to forget, no dashboard you'll check twice and abandon. If your family can forward a good-morning message, your family can use Savera.

  • Morning check-in summaries, without hovering
  • Dawa confirmations the moment they happen
  • Voice notes delivered home and replies brought back
Where Savera is today

A working prototype, built with honest scope.

Savera is a final-year engineering project, designed and built in Kanpur by two students who call their own parents every day. It is not in stores. There is no pre-order button, because we're building the real thing first.

Everything on this page describes what the prototype does today or what we're building next, marked plainly.

  • ●Working today — Hindi voice conversation, daily check-ins, dawa reminders, family voice notes over WhatsApp.
  • ◐On the bench — prayers & bhajans, news, weather and stories, more Indian languages, gentler onboarding for first-time users.
  • ○One day — a Savera in every home where the phone rings less than it should.
